_Author's note_  
"Conflicts are not allowed". That saying was chosen to represent our order for a reason. Is life complicated? Why yes of course. Can things be interpreted a multitude of ways? Well that goes without saying. What should be written? Now that is the reason for the saying. You see foundations are to be solid. Immutable. Should we build our very civilisation on conflicts and vagaries we would tumble from our own weight. We need a history. We do not need a multitude of them. One consistent world view. Technicalities and nuance will leave us bickering 'ad infinitum' as the Old Empire used to say. Now in peace that's fine. In war, well you don't see a southern horde arguing about which handle is better to use during a raid. It may not seem like we are at war...but the documents do not lie. The very existence of the following shows one of the buboes of this upcoming conflict. I believe we have already seen a few of the combatants. To stop my rambling going 'ad infinitum' I shall explain why this document is an exception. We have chosen to write two accounts of the closing war. Simply because our order does not believe the war to be ended - just yet. So as 'Conflicts are not allowed' half of this document is to be burned by the victor when this war draws to a close. Should I be long dead at this time. My author's note should also be burned. Can't have me explaining there was another side."  
- Signator Paxwell
  

*War of the Bookkeepers as accounted by Captain of the Vanguard of Balewind Alisdair Wiprecht*

Honour and truth stands above all things it looks down on even the titans above. Never is this more true then when relating to people of power. A claim was made by the denizens of Traen. As the offended party both my father Unerich and I had to respond. Chances of redemption were offered to their people and no act was to be carried out should redactions be made. No response was given so action was needed. We traveled to the bishopric of Carding, peacefully after seeking their neutral council. They spoke of neutrality and wished to stay out of the affairs of politics. Next we travelled back to Tri-Point to seek council with the Eunians - upon hearing our thoughts they rallied with us. Seeking Traen's foul deed undone. We entered Poll's Pass and met no resistance from the farmers who stood idly by. We remained in our tents and sought no protection from winter winds in the homes of the locals. Not wishing to drag innocents into conflict. In the night we were assaulted by the Count of Traen's men. Houses were burned in an attempt to drive us out. Many of us, having resided in the mountains nearby (seeking to leave the town to it's own devices), rallied to spare the innocent lives. The battle raged over the course of a few days and stalemates caused both parties to flee on occasion but never convincingly enough to eradicate the other. The Pale though wishing neutrality ultimately became a battleground, with Rivera and Hillwood siding with us, we did not seek to march through the free city of Mahbur. The Bishopric of Mt Celes preached inaction as did their brothers in Carding. The Glenns had been taken by the Traen, not with force but honied words. Bolund had been swayed by the honour of the other Counties that came with us. This was seen as an insult in Traens eyes. Bolund was home to the Elector. Him siding with us gave legitimacy not only to our cause by to my heritage. Fallsmarch had already been warring with Elurin so in a sense Bolund's hands were forced. During Midwinter and it's week of festivals an assault was made on Fallsmarch. Native men were currently sieging down Elurin when we were struck. Galahad Hoff's yeoman cavalry ravaged through our camp. Armed with my broadsword I and the men attempted to defend ourselves. Overrun many fled to the lands of Bolund. During the battle Galahad's horse knocked me to my back. On command its hoof crushed my right hand. The hard soil acted like an anvil to the iron strike of the horseshoe. The remainder of the war I spent commanding rather than fighting. Luckily, the Denburish had heard of our war - and it's reasons. Their healers, though unable to save ability in my hand stopped infection. They sent men and aid. Knowing this Traens forces marched to Bolund. The siege took months and only after the reinforcements from Denbur did the Bolunders sally forth. The war waged on for years. Castles being lost, taken and retaken. Only when the Kingsmen from Saustri intervened did the war stop. A compromise was made and I was told to regain my heritage with public deed. The Emperor Maxil IV said should I drive the brigands from my lands and those of my allies I would be re-introduced into my birthright. I am my fathers son. No scrap of paper written by a northerner will prove otherwise. My mother was faithful despite the Hoffs ramblings. I will regain my honour and gods willing take more than Galahads hand. Signed Alisdair Wiprecht  

_War of the Bookkeepers as accounted by Captain of the Vanguard of Cloverleaf Galahad Hoff_  
 
An offence, when truthful, should not be a cause for war. My father Thurar did not even speak the offence which triggered this invasion from the south. They demanded oaths and recompense for the neutral order of the Poll. Yes, we house them within our borders but we no more rule them than we do a person of the cloth - their oaths are above any lord or count. The clergy of Carding warned us of an incoming attack. We travelled south to defend the farmers of Poll's Pass. Fieldmen and Bookkeepers good guards do not make. As lords of them we sought only their protection. Having heard of their seizure of farmsteads we went to meet them in battle. Words can be solved with words but deeds can only be met in kind. The scouts, seeing torrents of smoke bilow forth, gave notice to the rest of the army. Despite the potential for danger our men rushed in to save the innocent lives. It was a diversion. They had used the innocent as bait. They descended from the mountains and exposed our rear. Bravely, we fought them back and it took days to rout them from their encampments on the mountainside. The Pale sought peace. Eunia having already sided with Fallsmarch caused Hillwood to concede - they had no means of support from us. As they joined with the Southerners Rivera soon fell to their might too. Were it not for the devoutness of Mt Celes, Mahbur would have fallen also. A stalemate was drawn where skirmishes lasted in the months. Elurin and Fallsmarch were at each others necks and Bolund had fallen to southern pretenders. We were in luck that the Glens honour remained in-tact. As skirmishes raged ravaging much of the central portion of the Pale the timer for the war reached years. A battle took place in Fallsmarch. In attempt to save Elurin we assaulted one of their camps -our disruption attempt to draw the native soldiers home. Many were wounded including Alisdar Wiprecht. We fought in footed combat. During a riposte I was able to drive him backward. A loose horse from their stables crushed his hand after he fell. The following siege of Bolund took months and only after Denbur intervened were we driven back. Ground changing hands with each season. The Emperor, on my fathers' request intervened. With his council the crown offered a deal to the people of the south. Should the brigands be cleared from Alisdair's lands and those of his allies, kinship can be reinstated to the bastard child of Unerich Wiprecht - or more appropriately his wife's bastard. Though my father did not see the deal as fair - blood being important. The good such a deal would bring to the people of Fallsmarch - after these years of war was too tempting an offer. So we allowed the lie for the greater peace. Alisdair is no noble. If he can prove to be noble in deed perhaps we can tolerate such a lie. Despite his reckless abandon for the safety of his people. I hope healers address his hand. A true Count would need to be able to fight. 
Signed Galahad Hoff
